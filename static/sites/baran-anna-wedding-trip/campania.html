Wed 14 October

The Naples day

Nine ways to do Pompeii, Vesuvius and Herculaneum, priced all-in — and the reason every group tour undoes the date this trip was built around.

The finding that shapes this page

Every group tour caps Pompeii at about two hours. Without exception, across everything on Viator. Wednesday 14 October was chosen because it is the last day of Pompeii’s summer timetable — closing 19:00 rather than 17:00, last entry 17:30 rather than 15:30. That two-hour tail is the reason the trip leaves Boston on the 7th. Book a group tour and the tail goes unused, and the 15th would have done just as well.

★ The good tours leave from Naples, not Rome

The Naples-departing tours are cheaper, far better reviewed and much shorter days — eight hours against twelve or thirteen. The best-reviewed thing in the entire field is a $75 Naples departure with 1,692 reviews at 4.8.

And you are taking the Frecciarossa to Naples anyway. Buying the train yourself and meeting a Naples tour at the station dominates every Rome package on price, rating and day length. The Rome ones are selling you a train ticket you can buy for €70 and a coach ride you do not want.

Book a private driver to meet the train — roughly €400–500 for the group, station to Vesuvius to Pompeii and back. The alternative is the Circumvesuviana, which is crowded, unreliable and pickpocketed, and you have two timed tickets and two children riding on it.

If the weather shuts Vesuvius, and it can, the day still works — go straight to Pompeii and add Herculaneum or the Villa dei Misteri. There is no second Wednesday.

What the listings do not tell you

“Discover Pompeii by High-Speed Train”, $377 — do not book this. It sells Mount Vesuvius in the title and the published itinerary goes to Naples instead. The Pompeii entry ticket is not included. It is non-refundable and non-changeable for any reason. It has seven reviews at 4.1, one of which says the train was not actually covered.

The $200 Herculaneum tour has a shopping stop. Fifty minutes at MaxiMall Pompeii, a shopping centre, is written into the itinerary — and the Pompeii portion is a string of ten-minute pauses. Otherwise a good tour: both entries and the return train are genuinely included, and it cancels to 24 hours.

Check what “included” means every time. Several Rome departures exclude the Pompeii entry, and the Naples three-site tour includes the Vesuvius ticket but not Pompeii or Herculaneum. That is about €36 a head back on.

Vesuvius is online-booking-only and shuts for wind. No tour can get you round that — if the mountain closes, it closes for the coach party too.

The money is a wash, so decide on the day. Six hours at Pompeii with a seven-year-old is an assumption worth testing — uneven stone, no shade, and it lands after three consecutive days of Rome. Two guided hours at Pompeii plus Herculaneum, which is smaller, far better preserved and much less walking, may be the better day even though it is less of the headline site.

⚠ But decide before booking anything else. If it is a group tour, Wednesday the 14th stops being special, and the Pompeii-hours argument that moved the whole trip a day earlier no longer applies.
