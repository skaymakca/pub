Reference · what is within reach

Tuscany, and what you could actually reach

Everything worth considering from the estate — what it is, how far it is, and whether there is a day for it. Most of this you will not do. That is what the list is for.

The constraint is not distance

Monaciano is better placed than Florence is. Siena is twenty minutes away. The whole of Chianti is inside forty-five. San Gimignano, Volterra, Montepulciano, Cortona and Arezzo all sit around an hour, and the Val d'Orcia at an hour and twenty. Nearly everything on this page is within ninety minutes of where you are sleeping. What you are short of is days.

The gap nobody has claimed

Sunday afternoon is free, and it points the right way. Brunch ends around half twelve; Cecchini's aperitivo is at 19:00 in Panzano, forty minutes north. That is five hours you have to spend travelling in that direction regardless — and the two best things in Chianti sit almost exactly on the line. Castello di Brolio is 18 minutes from the estate and 40 minutes on to Panzano; Badia a Coltibuono is 19 and 31. Either turns dead time into the wine afternoon you would otherwise never fit in.

What actually works with a seven- and an eleven-year-old

Tuscany is largely paintings and squares, which is a hard sell at that age. The things on this page that genuinely land are the ones you can climb into, climb up, or eat.

Underground. Orvieto's tunnels, the Pozzo di San Patrizio, the Etruscan labyrinth under Chiusi, the wine cellars cut into the rock at Montepulciano. Cool, dark, and nothing to keep quiet for.

Up. The Torre del Mangia over the Campo, Giotto's campanile, the walls at Monteriggioni and Montalcino. 400 steps is a feature, not a cost.

Water and fire. The steaming piazza at Bagno Vignoni and the free cascade below it.

Things that are frankly a bit gruesome. The Hell mosaic in the Florence Baptistery, Signorelli's demons at Orvieto, the sword in the stone above San Galgano.

Gelato as a destination. Dondoli in San Gimignano has won the world championship twice, and telling them that first makes the tower town worth the drive.

The one to watch is stacking two museum days back to back. The plan already puts the Accademia and the dome within eighteen hours of each other; a third fine-art stop on the Wednesday is where it would tip.
