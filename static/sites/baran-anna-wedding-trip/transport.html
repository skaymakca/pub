Cars, trains and the ZTL

Getting around

Where a car earns its place, where it is a liability, and the camera-enforced zones that make the difference expensive.

The shape of it

The car is a four-day rural instrument. It exists because the estate is in a field outside Castelnuovo Berardenga and because Chianti has no useful public transport. Every leg that touches a city is transit — and in all four cities you sleep in, a car is a liability rather than an asset.

The three decisions inside this

1. When the car starts — settled, and worth knowing why. Friday the 16th at Fiumicino. Not on arrival the previous Monday: none of the four Rome days want a car, and parking one runs €120–160 for the block plus restricted-zone exposure, for a vehicle that never moves. It starts at the airport on Friday because you are already going there to collect Zehra off the overnight — the pickup costs nothing in time.

2. When it stops — this one is live, and it rides on Tuesday.

Roughly a wash on money. ★ What decides it is luggage. Tuesday is a moving day — you check out of Florence and sleep in Bologna. In the car the bags sit in the boot while you are at Maranello. By train you would leave them at Bologna Centrale, go out to Modena and back, then collect — a third moving part on top of two changes and a museum shuttle that only runs every ninety minutes. If Tuesday is Ferrari, keep the car. If Tuesday is Florence, drop it Monday — you do not leave the city again until you leave for good.

3. Cecchini on Sunday night. Not really a decision. NCC either way, €130–160 for the round trip. The car cannot do that job.

The car itself

Duration4 daysone-way, both ends in Italy

The largest estate you can get, manual. An MPV runs roughly €700 against €300 for a big wagon, and the extra buys seats nobody sits in — there are five of you, so the third row is folded flat the whole trip either way. The only number that matters is boot volume with all five seats up.

Ask for an estate, not an SUV. The intuitive upgrade is a bigger SUV and at this size it is the wrong move — SUVs trade luggage space for ride height:

* The Tiguan only reaches 652 L with the rear bench slid forward, which costs the three people on it their legroom — so treat it as the lower figure. A big estate also handles Chianti lanes and Siena garages better than anything tall.

Watch the substitution. “Standard Wagon” can arrive as an Octavia Combi at 640 L or a Golf Variant nearer 600. Book the largest wagon class offered, read the “or similar” list, and push back at the desk if they produce a hatchback. Manual helps: it is the common fleet, so there is more to substitute from.

Then pack for it. Soft duffels over hard shells — the single biggest lever, because soft bags fill irregular space and hard cases waste it. Wedding clothes in a garment bag laid flat on top rather than a case of their own. Two of the five are children with small bags, which is what makes 690 L work at all.

Quote the one-way fee explicitly. Domestic Italian one-ways run from nothing to about €150 and vary more by agency than by distance — it often does not surface until late in the booking flow. Add Marina as second driver while you are there. Fuel full-to-full, and decline the desk prepay.

★ Drop at Florence airport, not the city office

Every rental office in Florence clusters on Borgo Ognissanti, which sits right on the edge of the restricted zone. There is a permitted approach — over the Arno at Ponte Amerigo Vespucci, then via di Melegnano, then left onto Borgo Ognissanti, because via di Melegnano is the only way to reach that junction without crossing a camera. Some agencies will absorb the entry charge if you return to one specific garage, from one specific street, within three hours.

That is a great many conditions to satisfy at the end of a long day, in traffic, in a city you do not know, having just driven from Siena.

Florence airport is fifteen minutes out and entirely outside the zone, and the T2 tram runs into the centre in about twenty minutes. It removes the only restricted-zone exposure on the whole trip. Any airport surcharge will be a fraction of one fine, and it turns a navigation problem into a non-problem.

The most expensive mistake available on this trip

A ZTL — zona a traffico limitato — is a camera-enforced perimeter around a historic centre. No barrier, no gate, nothing that stops you. A white circle with a red border, usually with a lit panel reading VARCO ATTIVO or NON ATTIVO. Cameras read the plate going in and again coming out.

One piece of luck: Florence’s summer night ZTL, which pushes Thursday-to-Saturday restrictions through to 3am, ran 2 April to 4 October 2026. It has expired before you get there.

Each camera is a separate violation, so one wrong loop looking for a hotel can produce three fines rather than one.

How the fine reaches you

You will not know at the time — nothing happens, no light flashes, no one stops you. Months later the comune notifies the rental company, which charges the card on file plus a €30–50 administrative fee for identifying you, and forwards the verbale. The usual lag is two to six months — long enough that the trip is a memory and disputing it is impractical.

Four rules

Do not trust the sat-nav. Google Maps routes straight through these zones by default. This is the single most common way visitors are fined.

Do not follow the car in front. It is a resident, a taxi or a delivery van — all whitelisted, none of which is you.

If a hotel is inside a zone, it must register your plate in advance. The hotel notifies the traffic police for a defined window, and that permission covers driving to the hotel and its parking — not driving around the centre. Ask before booking the room, not on arrival.

Park outside and walk in. Florence has Villa Costanza, a large park-and-ride at the T1 tram terminus. Siena has Santa Caterina and Il Campo, both with escalators up. Bologna: anything outside the ring.
