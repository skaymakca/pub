Fifteen days

Day 1Oct 8Thu
BOS → FRA

Evening departure, 17:45. Overnight to Frankfurt.

Day 2Oct 9Fri
Zadar

On the ground at 10:00, collected at the airport by Marina's family. No rental, no plan — the Croatia leg is theirs to shape.

Day 3–5Oct 10–12Sat–Mon
Zadar — family

Three full days with Marina's side. Not planned here and not needing to be.

Day 6Oct 13Tue
Zadar → Split → Rome → Orvieto

The transfer day, and it is a short one. Away from Zadar about 12:30, Split airport by 14:05. Wheels up 16:10, into Fiumicino 17:10. Italian car by 18:00, Orvieto by 19:30. Dinner on a hilltop in Umbria at eight.

The only hard number on the Croatia side: be at Split airport by 14:05. It is 1h35 from Zadar, however you cover it.

Day 7Oct 14Wed
Orvieto → Val d'Orcia

Orvieto in the morning: the striped Duomo, then the underground — 1,200 caves, wells and tunnels dug under the town over three thousand years. An hour, cool and dark, and the single best thing you can hand a kid on day two in Italy.

Then into the Val d'Orcia. Bagno Vignoni, where the piazza is a steaming Roman bath. San Quirico. Pienza for the evening.

Day 8Oct 15Thu
Montepulciano → Florence

Montepulciano in the morning — cellars cut into the rock under the main square. Then straight up to Florence, about two hours, arriving early afternoon.

Siena comes off this day — it is twenty minutes from where you are sleeping all weekend, so it has a better home on Monday. Montalcino goes too; it was the spare hill town.

The Accademia this afternoon for David — open until 18:50, and doing it today is what makes tomorrow survivable. Then Ponte Vecchio at dusk, dinner, early night.

Zehra lands FCO ~09:00, direct Frecciarossa to Firenze SMN (2h15, ~€25), meets you at the hotel

Day 9Oct 16Fri
Florence Monaciano · welcome 17:30

08:15 — the dome, first slot. 463 steps, no lift, the whole city underneath you at the top. The Baptistery doors are twenty minutes and right across the piazza.

Coffee, bags in the car, and out of Florence by 11:30. Monaciano is 1h 30m away — you are at the Siena end of Chianti, not the Florence end.

At the estate by about 13:00. Lunch there, unpack, walk the vineyard, change. 17:30 — pizza and spritzes.

With David done last night this is a comfortable day with four hours of slack. Done the other way round it was a scramble.

Dome 08:15 — name-matched, and the slot cannot be changed or refunded once issued

Day 10Oct 17Sat
THE WEDDING

The reason for all of it. Nothing else goes on this day.

Day 11Oct 18Sun
Wedding brunch Panzano — dinner

The wedding brunch takes the morning. That is the day's anchor and everything else works around it.

Afternoon free and deliberately empty — you will have eaten twice by dinner.

Officina della Bistecca at 19:30, aperitivo at 19:00 if you want the full run. Panzano is about fifty minutes north of Monaciano on the Chiantigiana.

Book a driver for this one. Wine is included, it runs three hours, and the SR222 back at eleven at night is unlit, narrow and full of switchbacks. An NCC for the round trip runs roughly €130–160 for five and removes the only genuinely unsafe hour on this itinerary.

Cecchini dinner seating, not lunch — the brunch takes 12:30. Single seating, book now.

Book the car and driver at the same time — the estate reception will have a regular firm

Day 12Oct 19Mon
Siena → Rome

Check out about 09:30 and drive twenty minutes into Siena — the single best thing about being at this end of Chianti.

Park in a garage outside the walls — Santa Caterina or Il Campo, both with escalators up into the centre — and leave the bags locked in the car. Then the Campo and the Duomo, which is the better cathedral of the two you will have seen, and a long lunch. All on foot, no ZTL anywhere near you.

Back to the car about 14:00, five minutes across town to drop it, then the direct bus to Rome — 2h 40m to Roma Tiburtina, €8–12, no changes. That beats driving ninety minutes to Florence for a train.

Evening in Rome with nothing booked. The Trevi Fountain and the Pantheon are both better after dark and both cost nothing to stand in front of.

Book the bus first and work backwards — the drop time has to suit the departure

Bags stay in the car until the drop. Do not count on left luggage for five people.

Day 13Oct 20Tue
Ancient Rome

Colosseum on a timed morning slot, then the Roman Forum and Palatine Hill — one ticket covers all three within 24 hours of your Colosseum time.

That is a great deal of walking on rough ground. Break it after the Forum. In the afternoon the Capitoline Museums sit right above it — the she-wolf, the bronze Marcus Aurelius, and the best view back down over the Forum. Then the Pantheon.

Colosseum tickets release exactly 30 days ahead — Sept 20, around 09:00 Rome time

Pantheon is free for under-18s but every visitor still needs a booked slot

Day 14Oct 21Wed
The Vatican

The Papal General Audience, 9am in St Peter's Square. Wednesday is the one morning it happens. Tickets are free but must be requested in advance.

Then St Peter's Basilica — Michelangelo's Pietà is just inside the door on the right, behind glass.

Afternoon: the Vatican Museums and the Sistine Chapel. Open to 20:00 with last entry at 18:00, so an afternoon slot is comfortable and much quieter than the morning scrum.

Papal Audience tickets — Prefecture of the Papal Household, or visitorsoffice@pnac.org

Collect the audience tickets 15:00–19:00 the day before

Day 15Oct 22Thu
FCO → BOS

Delta nonstop, 13:10 to 16:43. A real last morning in Rome first — keep it unticketed.
