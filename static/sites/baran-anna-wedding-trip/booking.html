What to buy, and when

In order, with the deadlines that actually bite.

The one unforgiving date

Sunday 20 September, around 09:00 Rome time. Colosseum tickets release exactly 30 days ahead and the good slots go within hours. Everything else on this page can slip a week; that cannot. Sources differ on 08:45 versus 09:00 — be sitting there at 08:40.

The car

Pick upRome Fiumicino (FCO)Tue Oct 13, ~18:00after the 17:10 landing and bags
DropSiena city officeMon Oct 19, ~14:00before the afternoon bus
Duration6 daysone-way, both ends in Italy

The largest estate you can get, manual. An MPV costs roughly €700 against €300 for a big wagon, and the extra is buying seats you will not sit in — there are five of you, so the third row is folded flat the entire trip either way. What you are actually shopping for is boot volume with all five seats up, and that is the only number that matters.

Ask for an estate, not an SUV. The intuitive upgrade is a bigger SUV and it is the wrong one at this size — SUVs trade luggage space for ride height:

* The Tiguan only reaches 652 L with the rear bench slid forward, which costs the three people sitting on it their legroom for two weeks — so treat it as the lower figure. A big estate also handles Chianti lanes and Siena garages better than anything tall.

Watch the substitution. "Standard Wagon" can arrive as an Octavia Combi at 640 L or a Golf Variant nearer 600 — book the largest wagon class the agency offers, read the "or similar" list, and push back at the desk if they produce a hatchback. Manual helps here too: it is the common fleet, so there is more to substitute from.

Then pack for it. Soft duffels over hard shells — the single biggest lever, because soft bags fill irregular space and hard cases waste it. Wedding clothes in a garment bag laid flat on top rather than a case of their own. Two of the five are kids with small bags, which is what makes 690 L work at all.

The one-way fee is the thing to quote explicitly. Domestic Italian one-ways run anywhere from nothing to about €150, and it varies more by agency than by distance — FCO to Naples has historically been around €40. It often does not surface until late in the booking flow. Add Marina as a second driver while you are there; there is a two-hour Val d'Orcia day and a 1h30 Florence run in this. Fuel policy full-to-full, and decline the desk's prepay.

Dropping in Siena — the bit with risk in it

Siena has no airport. These are small city offices, and Italian branches routinely close for a midday break and keep short afternoon hours. Plan a long lunch and then drop, and you can find the shutter down with a bus to catch.

All three sit outside the ZTL, as does the station — so there is no camera between you and any of them. Confirm the Monday afternoon hours when you book, and book the FlixBus first so the drop time is set by a real departure rather than a guess.

Two ways to lose money here

Buy from the official site, not the first search result. Paid search for "Colosseum tickets" is full of resellers holding separate allocations at €29+ against a €18 face value. The Colosseum moved off CoopCulture to ticketing.colosseo.it in April 2024, so older guides send you to the wrong place. The Duomo has publicly warned about the same thing — buy the Brunelleschi Pass at tickets.duomo.firenze.it and nowhere else.

The dome slot is not refundable and not changeable, and it is name-matched with ID checked at the door. Everything about Friday hangs off it, so book it only once the estate address and the drive time are confirmed.
