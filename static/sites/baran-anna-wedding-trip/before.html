Rome · Pompeii · the Vatican

Before the wedding

Three days in Rome with a volcano in the middle of them — what each thing is, how long it takes, and whether it works with a seven-year-old.

Three days, and they are not interchangeable

Tuesday is ancient Rome, Wednesday is Pompeii and Vesuvius, Thursday is the Vatican. Wednesday is fixed by Pompeii’s summer hours and cannot move. The other two can swap, but Thursday-for-Wednesday costs you two hours at Pompeii, which is the whole reason the trip leaves on the 7th.

Reading the kid column

great for them  climbable, underground, gruesome or edible they will cope  fine in a normal dose hard with kids  long, static, queue-heavy or genuinely unsuitable

Maja is seven and Luka is eleven, so these are read for that spread rather than for teenagers. The failure mode on this half of the trip is not any single stop — it is three consecutive days of walking on Roman stone. Build a real break into each afternoon.

Ancient Rome Tue 13 October

Colosseumgreat for themThe Roman amphitheatre — a 50,000-seat stone arena finished in AD 80 for gladiator fights and public spectacles.1h30. The 50,000-seat amphitheatre of AD 80, and it does the work by itself — you stand in it and the arithmetic of the place lands without a word of explanation. One ticket also covers the Forum and Palatine within 24 hours. €18 + €2.ticketing.colosseo.itmap
Roman Forumthey will copeThe ruined civic centre of ancient Rome: the market square, law courts, senate house and temples the republic and empire were actually run from.1h30. The civic centre of the republic and empire. Magnificent if you know what you are looking at and a field of broken columns if you do not — this is the one stop on the trip where a guide or a good audio tour changes the day for a child.ticketing.colosseo.itmap
Palatine Hillthey will copeThe hill above the Forum where the emperors built their palaces — ruined imperial residences, gardens and a view down over the Forum.1h. The hill where the emperors actually lived and where the founding myth puts Romulus. Same ticket. First thing to drop if legs are going — and on a three-day Rome block, legs will be going.ticketing.colosseo.itmap
Capitoline Museumsthey will copeTwo Renaissance palaces on the Capitoline hill holding Rome’s ancient sculpture collection — founded 1471 and the oldest public museum anywhere.1h30. The world’s oldest public museum. The bronze she-wolf, the original equestrian Marcus Aurelius, a giant marble hand and foot in the courtyard that children photograph themselves against, and a terrace looking back over the Forum you just walked.museicapitolini.orgmap
Pantheongreat for themA Roman temple of AD 126, still standing intact and still roofed by its original concrete dome with a hole open to the sky.30 min. Nearly two thousand years old and still the largest unreinforced concrete dome on earth, with the oculus open to the sky — when it rains, it rains inside, and there are drains in the floor for it. Short, astonishing, over before anyone gets bored. Free for under-18s, but every visitor needs a booked slot.portale.museiitaliani.itmap
Trevi Fountaingreat for themAn enormous baroque fountain filling one end of a small square, fed by an aqueduct the Romans built in 19 BC.20 min. Baroque, enormous, jammed at every hour and genuinely better late at night. Free, and the coin-throwing is the point when you are seven.—map
Piazza Navonagreat for themA long oval square built directly on top of a Roman chariot stadium, lined with cafes and centred on a Bernini fountain.45 min. Built on the footprint of a Roman stadium, which is why it is that shape. Bernini’s Four Rivers fountain in the middle, street artists, and gelato. The easiest hour in Rome with children.—map
Bocca della Veritàgreat for themA carved marble face, probably an ancient drain cover, set in a church portico. Legend says it bites the hand of a liar.20 min + a queue. The drain cover that bites liars, in the portico of Santa Maria in Cosmedin. Pure tourist theatre and completely irresistible at that age. Free, but the queue can run 30 minutes.—map
Borghese Galleryhard with kidsA cardinal’s private villa turned art museum, holding the finest Bernini sculptures anywhere plus Caravaggio and Titian.2h, strictly timed. Bernini’s Apollo and Daphne and Persephone — marble that behaves like flesh, and arguably the best sculpture in Rome. Two-hour entry slots, booked well ahead, no re-entry. Superb, and the wrong shape for this trip.galleriaborghese.beniculturali.itmap

Pompeii and Vesuvius Wed 14 October — the fixed day

WhatWith the kidsWhat it isBookMap
Mount Vesuvius — Gran Conothey will copeThe volcano that buried Pompeii in AD 79, still active. You climb the last stretch on foot and walk part of the crater rim.2h from the car park. A shuttle climbs to 1,000 m, then a 30-minute walk up loose gravel to the crater rim, the rim walk itself, and 30 minutes down. Steep, exposed and windy in October — proper shoes, not trainers. The pay-off is looking into the thing that did what you are about to go and see. October hours 09:00–16:00, online booking only, suspended in bad weather.vesuviopark.vivaticket.itmap
Pompeii — the sitegreat for themAn entire Roman town of 11,000 people, buried by ash in AD 79 and dug out largely intact — streets, shops, houses and all.4–6h. An entire Roman town with its roof off: streets with cart ruts worn into the stone, bars with the jars still set into the counters, election graffiti on the walls. Children get this immediately in a way they do not get the Forum. Closes 19:00 on the 14th; 17:00 from the 15th. €20, and there is no under-18 exemption for non-EU visitors.pompeiisites.orgmap
The body castshard with kidsPlaster figures made by pouring into the cavities the victims’ bodies left in the hardened ash. They are people, in the positions they died in.Plaster casts poured into the voids left by the dead, in the Antiquarium and at the Orto dei Fuggiaschi. Decide before you go whether the seven-year-old sees these. They are people, in the positions they died in, and they are not signposted as a separate thing you can opt out of once you are in the room.—map
Villa dei Misterithey will copeA large Roman country house on the edge of Pompeii, famous for one room frescoed floor to ceiling with a life-size initiation rite.45 min. At the far edge of the site, which is why most people miss it. The frescoes are the best-preserved in the Roman world — a life-size initiation cycle on a red ground. Adds a 15-minute walk each way.—map
Herculaneum — the alternativegreat for themA second town killed by the same eruption. Smaller and richer than Pompeii, and buried differently — so upper floors, wooden doors and furniture survive.2–3h. Smaller than Pompeii, much better preserved — upper floors, wooden doors, carbonised furniture — and far less walking. If the day looks too long for Maja, this is the swap, and it is next to the Vesuvius shuttle at Ercolano rather than a separate leg.ercolano.beniculturali.itmap
Naples — Archaeological Museumhard with kidsThe museum in Naples holding everything portable that was dug out of Pompeii and Herculaneum — the mosaics, frescoes and bronzes are all here, not on site.2h+. Where everything portable from Pompeii actually went: the mosaics, the bronzes, the Farnese Hercules. The right way to understand the site — and there is no room for it on a day that already has a volcano in it.mann-napoli.itmap

The Vatican Thu 15 October

WhatWith the kidsWhat it isBookMap
St Peter’s Basilicathey will copeThe largest church in the world, built over the tomb of St Peter and finished in 1626 by Michelangelo, Bramante and Bernini between them.1h. The largest church in the world, and the scale is the experience — there are markers in the floor showing where smaller cathedrals would end. Michelangelo’s Pietà is immediately inside on the right, behind glass. Entry is free; book only to skip the security queue. Shoulders and knees covered, children included.basilicasanpietro.vamap
Dome of St Peter’sgreat for themThe climb up inside Michelangelo’s dome to the lantern at the very top, coming out on a walkway above Rome.1h. 551 steps, or a lift to the roof and then 320. The upper stretch runs between the inner and outer shells and leans with the curve of the dome, which is the bit children remember. Open today — it is shut on Wednesdays, which is the quiet reward for giving up the Papal Audience.basilicasanpietro.vamap
Vatican Museumshard with kidsThe popes’ art collection, accumulated over five centuries — several miles of galleries walked one-way, ending at the Sistine Chapel.3h minimum. Miles of galleries, one-way, shoulder to shoulder, with no shortcut to the end. Extraordinary, and the single hardest thing on this trip for a seven-year-old. Book the afternoon — open to 20:00, last entry 18:00, and markedly quieter than the morning crush.museivaticani.vamap
Sistine Chapelthey will copeThe pope’s private chapel, with Michelangelo’s ceiling and Last Judgment covering it. It is where popes are elected.30 min. At the end of the Museums and only reachable through them. Silence is enforced and no photography is allowed. Show them the ceiling in a book first — knowing what to look for is the difference between fifteen minutes of wonder and fifteen minutes of a sore neck.museivaticani.vamap
Castel Sant’Angelogreat for themA drum-shaped fortress on the Tiber — built as the emperor Hadrian’s tomb in AD 139, later a castle and papal refuge, now a museum with a rooftop terrace.1h30. Hadrian’s tomb, then a fortress, then a papal bolt-hole — with the passetto, the escape corridor running along the top of the wall from the Vatican. Spiral ramps, battlements, cannon and a terrace over the river. The best-value hour in Rome for children, and ten minutes from St Peter’s.castelsantangelo.cultura.gov.itmap
Papal General Audience — not on this tripthey will copeThe pope’s weekly public appearance in St Peter’s Square: a short address and blessing to the crowd, free to attend.Wednesday mornings at 10:00 when the Pope is in Rome. Free, requested at least two weeks ahead through the Prefecture or the US bishops’ office, collected the Tuesday before. Wednesday belongs to Pompeii, and the October 2026 calendar is not published yet anyway — audiences are not held every week.visitorsoffice@pnac.orgmap

The Naples day

Wednesday has a page of its own — the Naples day — because there are nine ways to do it, they differ by a factor of four in price, and every group tour caps Pompeii at two hours against the six this date was chosen for.
