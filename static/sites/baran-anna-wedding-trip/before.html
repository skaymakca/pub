Rome · Pompeii · the Vatican

Before the wedding

Three days in Rome with a volcano in the middle of them — what each thing is, how long it takes, and whether it works with a seven-year-old.

Three days, and they are not interchangeable

Tuesday is ancient Rome, Wednesday is Pompeii and Vesuvius, Thursday is the Vatican. Wednesday is fixed by Pompeii’s summer hours and cannot move. The other two can swap, but Thursday-for-Wednesday costs you two hours at Pompeii, which is the whole reason the trip leaves on the 7th.

Reading the kid column

great for them  climbable, underground, gruesome or edible they will cope  fine in a normal dose hard with kids  long, static, queue-heavy or genuinely unsuitable

Maja is seven and Luka is eleven, so these are read for that spread rather than for teenagers. The failure mode on this half of the trip is not any single stop — it is three consecutive days of walking on Roman stone. Build a real break into each afternoon.

Ancient Rome Tue 13 October

WhatWith the kidsWhat it isBookMap
Colosseumgreat for them1h30. The 50,000-seat amphitheatre of AD 80, and it does the work by itself — you stand in it and the arithmetic of the place lands without a word of explanation. One ticket also covers the Forum and Palatine within 24 hours. €18 + €2.ticketing.colosseo.itmap
Roman Forumthey will cope1h30. The civic centre of the republic and empire. Magnificent if you know what you are looking at and a field of broken columns if you do not — this is the one stop on the trip where a guide or a good audio tour changes the day for a child.ticketing.colosseo.itmap
Palatine Hillthey will cope1h. The hill where the emperors actually lived and where the founding myth puts Romulus. Same ticket. First thing to drop if legs are going — and on a three-day Rome block, legs will be going.ticketing.colosseo.itmap
Capitoline Museumsthey will cope1h30. The world’s oldest public museum. The bronze she-wolf, the original equestrian Marcus Aurelius, a giant marble hand and foot in the courtyard that children photograph themselves against, and a terrace looking back over the Forum you just walked.museicapitolini.orgmap
Pantheongreat for them30 min. Nearly two thousand years old and still the largest unreinforced concrete dome on earth, with the oculus open to the sky — when it rains, it rains inside, and there are drains in the floor for it. Short, astonishing, over before anyone gets bored. Free for under-18s, but every visitor needs a booked slot.portale.museiitaliani.itmap
Trevi Fountaingreat for them20 min. Baroque, enormous, jammed at every hour and genuinely better late at night. Free, and the coin-throwing is the point when you are seven.—map
Piazza Navonagreat for them45 min. Built on the footprint of a Roman stadium, which is why it is that shape. Bernini’s Four Rivers fountain in the middle, street artists, and gelato. The easiest hour in Rome with children.—map
Bocca della Veritàgreat for them20 min + a queue. The drain cover that bites liars, in the portico of Santa Maria in Cosmedin. Pure tourist theatre and completely irresistible at that age. Free, but the queue can run 30 minutes.—map
Borghese Galleryhard with kids2h, strictly timed. Bernini’s Apollo and Daphne and Persephone — marble that behaves like flesh, and arguably the best sculpture in Rome. Two-hour entry slots, booked well ahead, no re-entry. Superb, and the wrong shape for this trip.galleriaborghese.beniculturali.itmap

Pompeii and Vesuvius Wed 14 October — the fixed day

WhatWith the kidsWhat it isBookMap
Mount Vesuvius — Gran Conothey will cope2h from the car park. A shuttle climbs to 1,000 m, then a 30-minute walk up loose gravel to the crater rim, the rim walk itself, and 30 minutes down. Steep, exposed and windy in October — proper shoes, not trainers. The pay-off is looking into the thing that did what you are about to go and see. October hours 09:00–16:00, online booking only, suspended in bad weather.vesuviopark.vivaticket.itmap
Pompeii — the sitegreat for them4–6h. An entire Roman town with its roof off: streets with cart ruts worn into the stone, bars with the jars still set into the counters, election graffiti on the walls. Children get this immediately in a way they do not get the Forum. Closes 19:00 on the 14th; 17:00 from the 15th. €20, and there is no under-18 exemption for non-EU visitors.pompeiisites.orgmap
The body castshard with kidsPlaster casts poured into the voids left by the dead, in the Antiquarium and at the Orto dei Fuggiaschi. Decide before you go whether the seven-year-old sees these. They are people, in the positions they died in, and they are not signposted as a separate thing you can opt out of once you are in the room.—map
Villa dei Misterithey will cope45 min. At the far edge of the site, which is why most people miss it. The frescoes are the best-preserved in the Roman world — a life-size initiation cycle on a red ground. Adds a 15-minute walk each way.—map
Herculaneum — the alternativegreat for them2–3h. Smaller than Pompeii, much better preserved — upper floors, wooden doors, carbonised furniture — and far less walking. If the day looks too long for Maja, this is the swap, and it is next to the Vesuvius shuttle at Ercolano rather than a separate leg.ercolano.beniculturali.itmap
Naples — Archaeological Museumhard with kids2h+. Where everything portable from Pompeii actually went: the mosaics, the bronzes, the Farnese Hercules. The right way to understand the site — and there is no room for it on a day that already has a volcano in it.mann-napoli.itmap

The Vatican Thu 15 October

WhatWith the kidsWhat it isBookMap
St Peter’s Basilicathey will cope1h. The largest church in the world, and the scale is the experience — there are markers in the floor showing where smaller cathedrals would end. Michelangelo’s Pietà is immediately inside on the right, behind glass. Entry is free; book only to skip the security queue. Shoulders and knees covered, children included.basilicasanpietro.vamap
Dome of St Peter’sgreat for them1h. 551 steps, or a lift to the roof and then 320. The upper stretch runs between the inner and outer shells and leans with the curve of the dome, which is the bit children remember. Open today — it is shut on Wednesdays, which is the quiet reward for giving up the Papal Audience.basilicasanpietro.vamap
Vatican Museumshard with kids3h minimum. Miles of galleries, one-way, shoulder to shoulder, with no shortcut to the end. Extraordinary, and the single hardest thing on this trip for a seven-year-old. Book the afternoon — open to 20:00, last entry 18:00, and markedly quieter than the morning crush.museivaticani.vamap
Sistine Chapelthey will cope30 min. At the end of the Museums and only reachable through them. Silence is enforced and no photography is allowed. Show them the ceiling in a book first — knowing what to look for is the difference between fifteen minutes of wonder and fifteen minutes of a sore neck.museivaticani.vamap
Castel Sant’Angelogreat for them1h30. Hadrian’s tomb, then a fortress, then a papal bolt-hole — with the passetto, the escape corridor running along the top of the wall from the Vatican. Spiral ramps, battlements, cannon and a terrace over the river. The best-value hour in Rome for children, and ten minutes from St Peter’s.castelsantangelo.cultura.gov.itmap
Papal General Audience — not on this tripthey will copeWednesday mornings at 10:00 when the Pope is in Rome. Free, requested at least two weeks ahead through the Prefecture or the US bishops’ office, collected the Tuesday before. Wednesday belongs to Pompeii, and the October 2026 calendar is not published yet anyway — audiences are not held every week.visitorsoffice@pnac.orgmap

~21:30Back in Rome

Book a private driver to meet the train — roughly €400–500 for the group, station to Vesuvius to Pompeii and back. The alternative is the Circumvesuviana, which is crowded, unreliable and pickpocketed, and you have two timed tickets and two children riding on it.

Roughly €900–1,100 for the day with the driver: trains €250–450 for five booked early, Vesuvius €10 plus €12 shuttle each, Pompeii €20 each with no under-18 exemption. About €550 without the driver.

If the weather shuts Vesuvius, and it can, the day still works — go straight to Pompeii and add Herculaneum or the Villa dei Misteri. There is no second Wednesday.
