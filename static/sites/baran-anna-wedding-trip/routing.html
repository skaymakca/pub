The constraint

Why the trip runs this way

One seasonal airport decided the entire route. This is the reasoning, the alternatives that were priced, and where the trip can be cut.

What changed on 30 August

The plan was Croatia, then a slow drive north through Umbria to the wedding, then Rome at the end. It is now Croatia, Rome and Pompeii, the wedding, then a short Tuscan tail and home from Bologna. Three findings forced it, and each one inverted something the earlier plan was built on.

1. Pompeii’s calendar, not its distance, decides the trip

Pompeii runs two timetables and the changeover is mid-October:

SeasonOpensLast entryCloses
16 Mar – 14 Oct09:0017:3019:00
15 Oct – 15 Mar09:0015:3017:00

One day either side of the 14th is the difference between six hours on site and three. Everything else followed from wanting to land on the right side of that line: Rome moves ahead of the wedding, the outbound moves to the 7th, and the Umbrian corridor comes out to pay for it.

Driving to Pompeii from Tuscany was priced and rejected — the estate to Pompei Scavi is 442 km and 4h40, and on a checkout morning that buys about two hours on site for nearly eight in the car. From Rome it is a 06:50 train and no driving at all.

2. Zadar was only ever right on one date

Zadar’s Italian routes are Ryanair seasonals that end in autumn, which is what made the original plan fly in to Zadar and out through Split. That still holds — but the good transatlantic into Zadar turns out to exist on 8 October and essentially nowhere else:

3. Bologna is the way home, not Florence

Florence looks like the obvious answer and is the wrong one. Peretola’s runway is hemmed in by terrain and the motorway, and it diverts to Pisa or Bologna when the wind gets up — which is what mid-October brings. On a one-stop transatlantic a diversion costs the connection. Bologna is $3 cheaper, 41 minutes faster and on a proper runway.

And a date trap avoided: the Rome nonstop is $812 on the 20th but $546 on the 21st — a $266 swing per head on one day. Bologna is flat across both, so routing through it removes the exposure.

About $960 more, for a volcano, a Roman town and an extra day in Rome. The corridor is the bill — Orvieto’s caves, the steaming piazza at Bagno Vignoni, Pienza, Montepulciano’s cellars. That was the gentlest, least strenuous stretch of the old plan, and losing it makes this a harder trip as well as a bigger one.

The Papal Audience is not settled either way. It is the pope’s Wednesday-morning public appearance in St Peter’s Square — a drive through the crowd, a talk in Italian, greetings to the pilgrim groups, a blessing — not a Mass and not a meeting with him. The October 2026 calendar is unpublished and audiences are not held every Wednesday, so the decision waits until late September. See the Naples day for how the week rearranges if there is one.
