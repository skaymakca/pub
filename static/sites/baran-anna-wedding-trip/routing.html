The constraint

Why the trip runs this way

One seasonal airport decided the entire route. This is the reasoning, the alternatives that were priced, and where the trip can be cut.

Zadar is a summer airport

Zadar has six nonstop routes to Italy — Rome, Pisa, Bologna, both Milans, Bari. Every one is a Ryanair seasonal route that ends in September or October, and by the 13th they are gone. Checked against live fares, the difference is not marginal:

So: fly in to Zadar, drive out through Split. Inbound still works — BOS→ZAD is $578 with one stop in Frankfurt, 10h 15m, which is actually faster than BOS→SPU ($563, 14h 05m). Zadar to Split airport is 1h 35m down the A1.

The other half of it: Rome is the only clean Italian door from Croatia. Nothing flies Croatia→Tuscany. That is why the trip enters Italy at Fiumicino and drives north rather than landing near the wedding.

Option 1 wins on geography. The Croatia→Italy hop is a one-hour nonstop; in Option 2 the same move needs a train back to Rome because nothing flies Tuscany→Croatia, and the return from Croatia is one stop through Heathrow at 11–14 hours instead of the 9h33 Rome nonstop. Option 2 costs about $820 more for a worse route, and takes two more of Luka's games.

The one honest argument for Option 2 is Zehra — she would travel with everyone in both directions rather than meeting them in Florence.
