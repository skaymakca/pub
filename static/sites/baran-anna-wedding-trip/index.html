The trip in one paragraph

Fly into Split on 7 October and spend four days with Marina's family up in Zadar. On the 12th, back down to Split for the one-hour hop to Rome — and Rome comes first now: ancient Rome on the Tuesday, Pompeii and Vesuvius on the Wednesday, the Vatican on the Thursday. Friday you collect Zehra off the overnight, pick up the car and drive north to Tenuta di Monaciano near Siena for the welcome party, the wedding on Saturday, brunch and Dario Cecchini on Sunday. Monday is Siena in the morning and Florence from mid-afternoon, and home from Bologna on the 21st.

Five of us for the wedding: Sukru, Marina, Luka, Maja and Zehra. Four for Croatia — Zehra joins in Florence on the 15th.

Three things worth knowing up front

Pompeii’s summer hours end on 14 October, and that is why the trip leaves on the 7th. Through the 14th the site closes at 19:00; from the 15th it is 17:00 with a 15:30 last entry. Departing a day earlier than the old plan puts Pompeii on the last summer day — six hours on site instead of three — and buys a third Rome day at the same time. It costs nothing in school days.

Split, not Zadar. The seasonal-airport problem that shaped the original route only bites on 8 October. Move a day either side and Zadar becomes two stops and thirteen hours, while Split holds a single-stop at 11h10 for less than the Zadar fare. You were always flying out through Split anyway.

Rome first means the Umbrian corridor goes. Orvieto, the Val d’Orcia, Pienza, Bagno Vignoni and Montepulciano are all out. That was the gentlest part of the old plan, and it is the entire price of Pompeii and a proper Rome block.
