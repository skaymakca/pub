Chianti · Siena · Florence · Bologna

After the wedding

Sunday afternoon, Monday, and a Tuesday that is still undecided. Everything within reach, marked for how it goes with children.

Two and a half days, and one of them is still open

Sunday afternoon, Monday, and Tuesday. Sunday is free between the brunch and Cecchini and points north anyway. Monday is Siena in the morning and Florence from mid-afternoon. Tuesday — Luka’s birthday — is the one decision left on this trip: a full Florence day, or the Accademia and then Ferrari on the way to Bologna.

Reading the kid column

great for them  climbable, underground, gruesome or edible they will cope  fine in a normal dose hard with kids  long, static, queue-heavy or genuinely unsuitable

By this point they will have done three days of Rome and a wedding. Tuscany is largely paintings and squares, which is a hard sell on a full tank and a harder one on an empty one — the things below that land are the ones you climb into, climb up, or eat.

Ferrari is $43 cheaper and costs you the Uffizi. That is the whole trade. Wednesday morning is too short for either — you leave central Bologna about 11:15 — so it has to be Tuesday.

If it is Ferrari, decide about the car. Keeping it from Rome through to Bologna airport means driving Florence–Maranello–Bologna and a one-way drop fee. Dropping it in Florence on the Monday and going by train costs nothing extra and nobody has to park in Modena.

It is Luka’s eleventh birthday. That is not a tiebreaker I should be making.

What actually works with a seven- and an eleven-year-old

Up. The Torre del Mangia over the Campo, Brunelleschi’s dome, Giotto’s campanile, San Petronio’s terrace. Four hundred steps is a feature, not a cost, and it buys goodwill for the next hour of paintings.

Underground and indoors-but-strange. Santa Maria della Scala running four levels down into the hill, the cellars at Coltibuono, the anatomical theatre in Bologna.

Frankly a bit gruesome. The Hell mosaic in the Florence Baptistery, the skinless statues at the Archiginnasio. These are the ones they describe to people afterwards.

Edible. Cecchini as theatre, the Mercato Centrale, the Quadrilatero. Bologna is the best eating city in the country and it is where the trip ends, which is the right way round.

The thing to watch is stacking fine-art days. By Monday they will have done the Vatican Museums and three days of Rome. The Accademia is one hour and worth it; the Uffizi on top of that, in the same 48 hours, is where it tips — which is an argument for Ferrari that has nothing to do with cars.
